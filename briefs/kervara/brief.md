# Kervara Visual Truth Document

## Current concept direction

Kervara meets the shore of **Lake Dravenne**, the large lake in the supplied relief image. The capital is among the smallest of the major kingdoms’ capitals, with fewer than **200,000** inhabitants. About **100,000** more live in the countryside and smaller cities. Use the Dolomites for the mountain forms and an ancient architectural vocabulary. The royal palace and fortress are carved into bedrock, with open-air courts, yards and terraces. The [first shoreline studies](lake-dravenne-concepts/generation-prompts.md) explore the western or northwestern shore. The exact footprint and replacement map pin remain open.

## Book One and the existing relief

Kervara is an inland fortress-city at the eastern end of the Ventara canyon, in the mountain basin that gives the kingdom its name. Its clearest silhouette is the rising canyon road, a working gate set into the ascent, and the mountain behind it. The Book One chapters give the fortress a public face at the gate, a working life in its wards and yards, a domestic life in its cold upper passages, and a royal burial place down inside the mountain. Use the Known World relief to place that sequence. [Corwin I, 5–11 and 85–99] [Corwin III, 3–9] [Hale I, 47–71] [Atlas Entry]

## The high country above the Long Traverse

The ride from the fortress to Warrick’s hunting ground takes three days. A cart-track narrows to a bridle path and then to a rough way for riders and mules. The long, low lodge stands at the last true timber before the grass. It is built of unmortared stone, with a hearth at each end, a yard and a stable. Above it are long grass, a ridge that catches the morning sun, a final climb, and a narrow shelf across a scree face. The shelf runs about sixty yards, pinches to a horse’s width, and holds white frost over hidden ice in the shade. The grass and exposed stone should carry the silhouette, with frost strongest in shade in the late-autumn scene. Harrow describes an old path within an hour’s walk of the lodge that emerges above the shelf. Its location on the relief is open. [Corwin IV, 89–105] [Corwin V, 49–109] [Selia II, 47–51]

## Concept image briefs

These are proposed compositions. Stone beyond the attested granite gate and lodge dry stone, roof materials, wall profiles, and the density of the lower town can be tested across variants.

1. **Regional aerial.** Match the supplied oblique terrain or start from the registered overhead. Place the city on Lake Dravenne and show the ridge-backed basin, western exit, and regional lakes. Give the open basin enough room to read at the same scale as the surrounding relief.
2. **Canyon approach and capital silhouette.** View the fortress from below the last bend. Use the road grade, porters or ore carts, garrison traffic, the inhabited lower town, and grey dusk to give the site scale. A possible layout puts the principal wards on rising ground behind the outer gate and leaves a circulation terrace where the relief supports it.
3. **The two gates.** Pair an outward-facing coronation at the windy outer gateway with a ground-level view into the inner ward during the wedding. Let worn granite, human-operated leaves, hinge, bar, and enough standing room carry the architecture. A dawn garrison yard offers the same city on an ordinary day.
4. **A section through the mountain.** Trace the public gate and upper stair into the royal passage, then descend to the torchlit Deep Gallery. Treat that connection as a section study. The manuscript fixes the gallery below the fortress and the chapel off the King’s Chamber, with the route between those spaces open.
5. **The high lodge.** Frame the dry-stone lodge against the last timber and rising grass, with the scree shelf as a separate detail view. Use early snow threats, persistent shade frost and an exposed ridge to put this scene at a different elevation and season from the capital’s spring thaw.

## Open visual decisions

- **LORE? City form.** Exact footprint, inhabited lower-town extent and density, wall circuit, towers, roof material and color, street finish, civic districts, insignia, and the shape of the outer gate. The manuscript’s crowd counts describe particular events.
- **LORE? Terrain registration.** Exact gate bearings and slope breaks, city frontage on the Thennara, local stream channel and bridges, mine and pasture locations, Vantis, the Long Traverse post and lodge, and the small unnamed waterbody in the screenshot. Keep the imported lakes and ridges anchored while those decisions are made.
- **LORE? Lake and water imagery.** The regional description gives Lake Dravenne an approximate **2,430 m** surface and places it south of the capital. It leaves the Aspera source phrased as the Eastern Ranges or Lake Dravenne. Verify the baked shoreline and drainage before drawing a city-to-lake water connection or a view from a particular terrace. - **Illustration check.** Chapter 32’s embedded coronation engraving depicts a throne dais. Use the prose’s exposed outer gateway for the scene geography. Chapter 40’s engraving can inform the close-up vocabulary of paper, seals, and lamplight. Its banner and decorated furniture have illustration status.
